# WO-2 — Inverse-Case Search: Costed, Declined Cross-Holder Signal Aggregation

**Author:** Manus AI
**Search completed:** 19 September 2026
**Result:** **Null within the bounded search.**

## Conclusion

I did **not identify a qualifying inverse case** in the official accident-investigation, audit-follow-up, regulatory-docket, and academic sources opened for this search. A qualifying record needed to establish all four elements in the same documented decision:

1. the proposed function had to **combine signals held by more than one person, unit, or source** into a risk-relevant view;
2. the organization or regulator had to **record a cost** for that aggregation function, rather than only call it expensive or unfunded;
3. the record had to state the **risk or harm the function was intended to reduce**; and
4. the organization or regulator had to **deliberately decline the function**.

The closest source is the National Transportation Safety Board’s (NTSB’s) 2026 Potomac collision report. It documents an Army flight-data programme, Military Flight Operations Quality Assurance (MFOQA), that is plainly a signal-analysis function and that the Army had not implemented. The report states that the programme was an “unfunded requirement” and quotes an official who said the Office of the Secretary of Defense could not support it with resources. However, neither the report nor the governing Defense instruction supplies the **actual cost estimate**, benefit estimate, or a documented component-head decision applying the instruction’s cost-benefit exclusion. It is therefore a **promising lead, not a true inverse case**.[1] [2]

This null finding is limited to the sources, databases, query families, and candidate records stated below. It does **not** show that no such case exists in government, industry, or the academic literature.

## Why the threshold was strict

The target is not simply a rejected safety recommendation. It is an inverse test of the WO-2 mechanism: a decision-maker knew that a designated cross-holder aggregation function could reduce a specified risk, put a price on that function, and then chose not to provide it. A generic statement that a recommendation was costly, a decision with no traceable rationale, or a cost-benefit assessment of one device does not test that mechanism.

In particular, a function is treated as **cross-holder aggregation** only if the record shows that it joins relevant fragments from distinct sources, organizations, or functions. A system that analyzes a single instrument stream or adds one physical safety device is not enough merely because its output is later reviewed by multiple people.

## Closest candidate: Army MFOQA after the Potomac collision

The governing Department of Defense instruction makes MFOQA a serious candidate at the **function** level. It directs Aviation Hazard Identification and Risk Assessment Programs to use flight-recorder, observation, self-reporting, and other data; collect identity-protected aggregate data; identify trends, human-factor issues, material deficiencies, and mishap precursors; and distribute results to operations, training, maintenance, safety, and engineering representatives. It also requires analysis to identify negative trends and mishap precursors, followed by risk assessment and mitigation.[2]

> “Program for MFOQA capability for existing aircraft, unless DoD Component cost-benefit analysis demonstrates a need for exclusion.” — DoDI 6055.19, §3.2.b.(3).[2]

The NTSB found that the Army’s 12th Aviation Battalion (TAAB) had no MFOQA programme at the time of the accident. It could not automatically identify altitude exceedances, near-midair collisions, or deviations. The Board later described the need for a “funded and institutionalized MFOQA capability” to detect emerging operational-risk trends.[1]

The same NTSB report says that the governing instruction allowed an exclusion if a cost-benefit analysis found MFOQA not cost-effective, and records testimony that MFOQA was an “unfunded requirement” for which Army personnel sought Office of the Secretary of Defense resources unsuccessfully.[1] That establishes a real risk-reduction function and an apparent funding non-provision, but it does not establish the requested inverse case:

| Required element | Evidence found | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-holder signal aggregation | The instruction requires aggregation of multiple data types and distribution of trend analysis to operations, training, maintenance, safety, and engineering. | **Present.** |
| Risk reduced | Hazardous trends and mishap precursors; the TAAB lacked automated detection of altitude exceedances, near-midair collisions, and deviations. | **Present.** |
| Recorded cost | “Unfunded requirement” and an unsuccessful resource request only. No dollar amount, lifecycle cost, or identified costed alternative was located in the opened primary records. | **Absent.** |
| Deliberate decline based on the recorded analysis | The instruction permits exclusions, but the opened record does not show that the Army component head performed and adopted a MFOQA cost-benefit exclusion. Resource support was not provided, but the decision record and rationale are not supplied. | **Unclear.** |

Accordingly, treating MFOQA as a positive case would overstate the evidence. It is neither an undocumented generic recommendation nor a single physical control; it is a close **partial** match whose missing decision-analysis document is dispositive.

## Excluded records and why they are not inverse cases

### Generic rejected recommendations and single-control cost decisions

A Government Accountability Office (GAO) review of NTSB recommendations states that cost can contribute to agencies’ nonimplementation decisions. Its example is the Federal Aviation Administration’s refusal to require two complete aircraft audio/data recorder systems after judging the installation’s cost excessive. That is documented nonimplementation, but it is a cost-benefit judgment about a **single recording/control configuration**, not a cross-holder signal-aggregation function. GAO also says NTSB itself did not determine costs associated with the benefits of a particular recommendation.[3]

The NTSB Potomac report also records prior proposals to move a helicopter route and add chart hotspots. The Board could not obtain documentation of the route-change requests or responses; the chart-hotspot proposal was not adopted because the term did not fit the chart specification. These are not a costed aggregation decision and cannot be promoted into one.[1]

### Documented aggregation cost without deliberate rejection

GAO’s Homeland Security Information Network (HSIN) audit describes an unmistakably cross-boundary information-sharing system. The Department of Homeland Security reported **$611.8 million** for development, operation, and maintenance of 11 networks and HSIN in fiscal years 2005–2006. GAO linked inadequate coordination to the risk that information would not be effectively shared and that capabilities would be duplicated. But DHS had deployed HSIN, was planning integration and coordination actions, and agreed to GAO’s recommendations. This is evidence about the cost and risk of an implemented/under-improvement information-sharing architecture, **not a deliberate decision to decline it**.[4]

PHMSA’s 2022 gas-transmission rule is likewise a nonmatch in the opposite direction. It explains that the San Bruno failure followed an integrity-management programme based on incomplete and inaccurate information, and its final rule **required** operators to integrate analyzed information and validate data to the maximum extent practical. Commenters raised costs of specialized expertise, new data sets, and database-management systems, but the regulator adopted the integration requirement. It is evidence that the target function can be regulated, not an inverse case.[5]

### Regulatory proposals or broad deregulatory aggregates

The EPA’s 2019 Risk Management Program reconsideration rescinded several provisions, including information-availability requirements, in a final rule. Its public description identifies the policy basis as choosing greater compliance at a subset of facilities rather than additional requirements across a wider population.[6] This does not qualify because the rescinded information-availability provisions concern public access and facility disclosure, rather than a demonstrated function that aggregates distributed pre-event hazard signals for a responsible risk owner.

EPA’s 2026 *Common Sense* proposal is closer to an explicit cost record: it proposes to rescind or modify various accident-prevention, employee-participation, information-availability, and documentation provisions, and estimates total annualized savings of about **$234.7–$241.9 million**. It is still a **proposal**, not a completed deliberate decline. More importantly, its total is a package-level regulatory-burden estimate. It does not isolate the cost and risk-reduction effect of a cross-holder aggregation function. It is therefore not counted.[7]

### Academic analysis with the wrong direction of decision

The peer-reviewed Cardiff violence-prevention study is strong evidence that multi-organization signal aggregation can be costed. It evaluated anonymised information sharing among health services, police, and local government; reported setup costs of £107,769 and annual operating costs of £210,433; and estimated £6.9 million in reduced violence costs in 2007. But the programme was implemented and studied as a beneficial intervention, not deliberately rejected. It is useful contrast evidence, not an inverse case.[8]

The exact search strategy emphasized synonyms for the core function—**aggregation, integration, information sharing, data sharing, risk analysis, trend analysis, and signal joining**—and for the decision—**declined, rejected, rescinded, not adopted, not implemented, unfunded, and cost-benefit**. This reduces, but cannot eliminate, the risk that a relevant record uses an unsearched vocabulary.

## Limits and next verification step

This is a bounded null, not a universal negative. Several limitations matter. First, accident-investigation records often describe a missing aggregation function and a corrective recommendation but do not contain the recipient’s budget analysis. Second, regulatory analyses commonly cost a whole rule, not one signal-joining function, so even a genuine non-adoption may fail the isolatable-cost test. Third, some underlying military and agency budget documents can be restricted or not indexed in public search.

The most efficient next step is therefore **not** another broad search. It is a targeted records search for the Army/DoD MFOQA cost-benefit analysis or component-head exclusion determination referenced by DoDI 6055.19, plus the resource-request response referred to in NTSB AIR-26-02. If those documents show a priced MFOQA capability, its risk-reduction logic, and a final nonimplementation determination, they could convert the partial MFOQA lead into a true inverse case. Until then, it should remain excluded.
