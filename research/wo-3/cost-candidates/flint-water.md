# Flint water corrosion control — WO-3 cost-boundary evidence record

**Candidate ID:** `flint-water-corrosion-control`
**Strict five-gate result:** **Does not qualify.** Gates 1, 2, 4, and 5 pass at their stated evidentiary limits, but gate 3 fails. Because the preregistered rule requires all five gates, this candidate is not a qualified WO-3 cost-boundary case.[1]

## Bottom line

**OBSERVED.** The reviewed record documents a specific available control—orthophosphate corrosion-control treatment (CCT)—and a regulator-directed delay or non-requirement of that control when Flint changed to Flint River water. Authoritative investigations and technical studies connect that delay or absence to later lead-exposure risk and harm. The record also supports several downstream response and recovery **cost categories**.[2] [3] [4] [5]

**DERIVED.** The candidate nevertheless **does not qualify**. The inspected contemporaneous primary and investigative materials do not provide a dated, control-specific price, avoided cost, schedule value, or explicit cost rationale for deferring or not requiring orthophosphate/CCT. Costs of source selection, reconnection, engineering support, upgrades, or later recovery cannot be transferred to that distinct control decision.[1] [3]

**UNRESOLVED.** This is a bounded evidentiary null, not proof that no qualifying record ever existed. The candidate could qualify only if a contemporaneous, decision-linked CCT cost or rationale record is produced.

## Observed decision and risk path

**OBSERVED.** When Flint Water Treatment Plant staff asked about phosphate used by Detroit for corrosion control, MDEQ said the treatment decision would follow two six-month monitoring periods. The task force found that this interpretation resulted in the decision not to require CCT at the Flint River switch. EPA OIG independently found that MDEQ delayed CCT installation and that the delay prolonged residents' lead exposure.[2] [5]

**OBSERVED.** The technical mechanism is source-supported. The task force reports that the more corrosive source destroyed the protective scale supplied by earlier orthophosphate addition. The peer-reviewed case study describes the source switch without corrosion control and deterioration of lead-bearing corrosion layers; the technical review explains that absent inhibitors can destabilize protective scale. The review's qualification is material: Flint River water may have been corrosive enough that some action-level exceedances would still have occurred with phosphate.[2] [4] [6]

**DERIVED.** These sources establish a material omitted-control-to-risk/harm connection. They do not establish but-for prevention of every injury, a causal allocation of every recovery dollar to CCT, or a claim that CCT alone would have eliminated all lead exposure.

## Cost-boundary result

**DERIVED.** No decision-versus-downstream cost delta is reported. The required original-boundary value—an ex-ante, decision-linked CCT cost, saving, schedule value, or rationale—is absent from the reviewed record. The downstream side therefore remains a non-additive register of categories rather than a counterfactual total.

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** A future redraw would be possible only if a contemporaneous record both identifies a CCT decision and supplies its price, avoided cost, schedule value, or explicit cost rationale. It would then need a fixed decision date, a fixed reporting cut-off, a price basis, defined payer categories, and an auditable non-overlap rule before any comparison could be calculated. Even then, downstream spending could not be assigned to CCT without a separately supported causal-allocation method.

**PLANNED.** Any further source search should target dated MDEQ, Flint, emergency-manager, procurement, permit, engineering, or budget materials that expressly connect the cost, saving, schedule effect, or rationale for orthophosphate/CCT to the 2014 deferral or non-requirement. Retrospective estimates, general source-switch savings, reconnection costs, plant-upgrade costs, and the $171,000 engineering fee do not satisfy gate 3.

## Ontology and asymptote boundary

**UNRESOLVED / NOT APPLICABLE.** This is a cost-case evidence record, not an asymptote assessment. It reports no crossing-rate calculation, no zero-crossing test, no modelled or theoretical crossing claim, and no classification of Flint as terminal or nonterminal under the crossing-metric protocol.[1]

**OBSERVED.** The documented regulatory delay, later exposure, and response/recovery categories show specified historical burdens inside the declared Flint system and accounting frame. They support the narrower inference that an expanded accounting boundary contains real downstream categories of burden, while causal allocation of those categories to omitted CCT remains unmeasured.

**DERIVED.** Those observations do not empirically prove that every possible crossing for every system is nonzero. They also do not establish an exact-zero upper bound for any universal ontology claim. Under the protocol, an exact-zero conclusion would require a complete channel inventory and zero upper bounds for every enumerated channel, quantity, expected quantity, probability, and dependency within a specified boundary and horizon; the literal all-time claim may instead require model-level proof.[1]

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** No asymptote receives a nonterminal classification in this record. Such a classification may be retained only where validated positive evidence establishes a lower bound above zero, and only for that asymptote's explicitly declared system boundary, horizon, lifecycle phase, and evidence status.[1]

## Bounded falsifier

**UNRESOLVED.** The current reviewed record does not qualify Flint as a WO-3 cost case because gate 3 is not established. It could qualify only if a contemporaneous, decision-linked CCT cost or rationale record is produced. Failure to locate that record in this bounded review is not evidence that it never existed.
