# Evidence record — Deepwater Horizon / cancelled cement-evaluation log

**Candidate ID:** `deepwater-horizon-cancelled-cement-evaluation-log`
**Strict selection result:** **DOES NOT QUALIFY — not selected for the WO-3 cost-boundary study.**
**Assessment date:** 19 September 2026
**Currency convention:** Amounts are stated in the nominal U.S. dollars and source years reported by the cited materials. No inflation adjustment, present-value conversion, allocation, or aggregation has been performed.

## Result

**DERIVED — strict five-gate result.** This candidate does not qualify because **Gate 3 is not met under the protocol's literal requirement for contemporaneous ex-ante cost evidence**. The National Commission's later report records that the decision saved time and a US$128,000 fee, citing July 2010 testimony, but this review has not located a decision-time invoice, cancellation instruction, or contemporaneous BP/Schlumberger cost record.[1] That retrospective official source chain is strong evidence of the asserted rationale; it is not itself a contemporaneous decision record. The candidate therefore remains non-qualifying unless such a record is produced and evaluated.

**DERIVED — bounded cost null.** The US$128,000 reported fee must not be arithmetically compared with later spill-cost categories. No cited source allocates any downstream dollar amount to the cancelled log, and no valid causal model supporting such an allocation is presented here. Accordingly, the record has **no quantified downstream cost attributable to the cancelled-log decision**. This is a null of documented allocation, not a claim that the decision had zero real-world cost, risk, or consequence.

**UNRESOLVED — causation.** The authoritative log-specific causal record is materially conflicted. The 2011 federal Joint Investigation Team (JIT) found that annular cement had not failed and concluded that not performing the log was not a cause of the well failure.[2] In contrast, the 2014 Phase One federal-court findings held that the log would have shown misplaced cement, enabled remediation, and that the decision not to run it was a substantial cause of the blowout, explosion, and spill.[3] The court's substantial-cause finding is an authoritative connection to later harm for Gate 4; neither authority proves that running the log would certainly have prevented every harm or every later cost.

## Decision and control

### Control available — OBSERVED

A Schlumberger cement-evaluation-log crew and tools were positioned on the rig. The JIT explains that the log evaluated cement-job quality and whether annular cement had set successfully; an adverse result could lead to pumping additional cement to remediate the primary job.[2] The log was therefore a diagnostic-and-remedial control, not itself an additional physical barrier.

BP's technical practice called for a proven cement-evaluation technique when the top of cement was not 1,000 feet above a distinct permeable zone. Listed techniques included cement-evaluation logs, cement-column back pressure, and temperature logs. The JIT found that the decision tree instead relied on lift pressure and apparent lost returns, neither of which was on that list of proven techniques.[2] BP's own investigation separately reports that a formal risk assessment might have identified a cement-evaluation log as a mitigation option; that statement is not proof that the log would have prevented the incident.[5]

### Decision and immediate rationale — OBSERVED

On 20 April 2010, the BP Macondo well team used its pre-established decision tree, concluded that the cement placement had gone as planned from lift pressure and apparent lack of fluid loss, and decided not to run the cement-evaluation log.[2] The National Commission reports that BP sent the three-person Schlumberger team home on the 11:00 a.m. helicopter, “thus saving time and the $128,000 fee.”[1]

The US$128,000 is retained narrowly as the Commission's reported fee. It is not a verified total avoided cost, a vendor invoice, or a price that may be converted into a downstream-loss ratio.

### Duration and cost evidence — OBSERVED, mutually non-identical

The federal court records testimony that the log would have taken approximately 10–12 hours. It also records BP testimony of approximately US$128,000 to run the log plus approximately US$400,000 for Schlumberger's time, alongside Schlumberger's estimate of less than US$200,000.[3] These are source-reported estimates with different stated scopes. They contradict any assertion that no primary source supplied a tied duration or cost figure, but they do not identify a common total cost or cure the absence of a located decision-time cost document. They must not be collapsed into one saving or added together.

## Causation, reach, and source conflicts

### Causation conflict — UNRESOLVED

The JIT found that the decision tree was inconsistent with BP guidance, but concluded that nonperformance of the log was not a cause of the well failure because the annular cement had not failed.[2] The 2014 federal court reached the contrary log-specific finding: it held that the log would have revealed misplaced cement, permitted remediation, and that the decision not to run it was a substantial cause of the blowout, explosion, and spill.[3] These conclusions are directly incompatible. The record preserves both their dates, authority, and stated scope. It does not elevate either into a claim that the log would certainly have prevented all injury, environmental harm, response activity, or expense.

### Log reach and diagnostic inference — MODELLED / CONDITIONAL

The final National Academies report states that the float-collar location would have prevented the log from investigating the lower cemented sections. It states more narrowly that the top of cement and the quality above the float collar could have been determined **if** the necessary foam-cement-evaluation software had been on board.[4] A separate 2010 interim Academies report conditionally states that the log could have helped reveal that cementing had not gone as planned and that meaningful flow oversight probably would have led to further diagnostic testing.[10] These final- and interim-report statements have different scope and must not be conflated.

### Cost-scope conflict — UNRESOLVED

The Commission reports a US$128,000 fee.[1] The court records BP testimony of approximately US$128,000 to run the log plus approximately US$400,000 for Schlumberger's time, while also recording Schlumberger's estimate of less than US$200,000.[3] The sources do not establish a common cost scope, price basis, or avoided-cost total. No ratio between these estimates and any spill-cost category is supported.

### Settlement-status correction — OBSERVED

The Department of Justice announced a US$20.8 billion proposed global civil resolution on 5 October 2015.[8] Judicial approval of the consent decree followed on 4 April 2016.[9] The distinction is retained because an announced or proposed resolution is not identical to judicial approval or a cash-flow total.

## Terminality, asymptote, and ontology limits

### Asymptote classification — UNRESOLVED / NOT APPLICABLE

This is a cost candidate, not an isolation asymptote. It provides no declared system `S`, boundary `B`, observation horizon `H`, lifecycle-stage inventory, crossing vector, or detection bounds. It therefore has **no asymptote classification** and no quantified crossing-rate result.

**OBSERVED — bounded nonterminal witness if a frame is declared.** If a defined boundary and horizon include the 20 April operational decision, the Schlumberger crew, or the documented public-response, corporate-accounting, and legal obligations, the sources establish human/institutional crossings and external dependency/exposure.[1] [6] [7] [8] Under the crossing metric, those positive observed witnesses reject terminality **only for that explicitly declared boundary and horizon**. They do not quantify a rate, establish a classification for a differently drawn boundary, or serve as an asymptote result.

### Exact-zero claim — MODELLED / CONDITIONAL

No empirical observation in this case can certify that every possible crossing is exactly zero. Observation can establish a positive event or a finite detection bound; absence of a detected further event does not establish a universal zero upper bound. A terminal-within-scope result would require a complete enumerated channel inventory and zero upper bounds for every channel for the stated boundary and finite horizon. The literal all-media, all-time zero-crossing claim would additionally require a model-conditional proof (or its equivalent), not observation alone.[11]

## Evidence boundary and next record needed

**PLANNED — requalification test.** To revisit Gate 3, locate and assess an authentic decision-time BP or Schlumberger invoice, cancellation instruction, decision record, or other contemporaneous document that records the ex-ante fee, schedule value, or explicit cost rationale. Such a document could satisfy the gate only if its provenance, timing, and cost scope are clear. It would not erase the JIT–court causation conflict or authorize downstream-cost allocation.

**UNRESOLVED — remaining evidentiary limits.** The log's actual diagnostic reach remains conditional on configuration, including the final Academies report's software condition and its lower-section limitation.[4] The record identifies no source-supported allocation of harm or cost to the cancelled log, no credible counterfactual that running it would have prevented all later losses, and no reconciliation permitting addition of the listed downstream categories.
