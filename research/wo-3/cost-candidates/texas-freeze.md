# WO-3 cost-boundary evidence record — Texas electric-grid winterization before the 2021 freeze

**Candidate ID:** `texas-freeze-pre-2021-winterization`
**Assessment:** **REJECTED — does not qualify for selection under the preregistered five-gate rule.**
**Author:** Manus AI
**Scope:** Texas/ERCOT electric-generation winterization between the February 2011 Southwest cold-weather event and Winter Storm Uri in February 2021. Dollar figures are retained in the source’s stated U.S. dollars and years; no inflation adjustment is made.

## Bottom line

**OBSERVED.** The cited record documents practicable physical and operating winterization controls. It also documents a reporting-centered Texas statutory regime after the 2011 event, substantial cold-weather and fuel-related generation failures during Uri, and downstream harm and financial-exposure categories. [1] [2] [3] [5] [8]

**UNRESOLVED — bounded cost null.** Within the cited 2011–pre-Uri public decision record, no located contemporaneous primary source ties a defined winterization control to an identified actor’s decision to decline, remove, defer, or weaken that control and records an ex-ante cost, saving, schedule value, or explicit cost rationale. This is a bounded finding about the located record, not proof that no such decision or document ever existed. Because Gates 2 and 3 fail, **the candidate does not qualify** as a WO-3 cost-boundary case.

**UNRESOLVED — source conflict.** A 2021 Federal Reserve Bank of Dallas article attributes a range of **$50,000–$500,000 per Texas gas plant, in 2011 dollars**, to FERC/NERC’s 2011 study. A full-text review of the cited 2011 FERC/NERC report does not locate that range; the primary report instead states qualitatively that needed fixes would in many cases not be “unduly expensive.” The later article is retained as a retrospective lead, not as contemporaneous or decision-specific Gate 3 evidence. No systemwide avoided-control-cost calculation is made. [1] [9]

## Declared boundary, horizon, and classification

**OBSERVED — decision frame.** The system is ERCOT-area electricity generation together with its dependency on gas production, processing, compression, and delivery. The decision boundary is the public regulatory and implementation boundary from 2011 through immediately before Uri: reporting and regulatory requirements and generator implementation lie inside it; electricity customers, water and medical services, other market participants, and public finance are outside it. The decision horizon is February 2011 through February 2021. The later Brazos bankruptcy process is included only as evidence of a financial-exposure category, not as an event occurring within the decision horizon or as a comparable control cost.

**OBSERVED — bounded nonterminal witness.** FERC/NERC reports that ERCOT ordered 20,000 MW of rolling blackouts and that more than 4.5 million Texans lost power during Uri. Under the declared boundary and Uri event frame, these facts establish a positive external dependency/exposure witness, `LD > 0`. The crossing metric therefore classifies the candidate as **nonterminal for that stated frame**. [8]

**UNRESOLVED — ontology limit.** This is not an asymptote, so an asymptote test is inapplicable. The positive `LD > 0` witness does not certify a complete channel inventory, an exact-zero upper bound in other channels, or a whole-life classification across construction, operation, dormancy, disposal, and afterlife. In particular, the decision/regulatory boundary is not a whole-life physical-system boundary. The record makes no claim that observation can establish that every possible crossing is exactly zero.

## Available controls and the pre-Uri decision record

**OBSERVED.** The 2011 FERC/NERC investigation describes winterization as physical protection and operating preparation. Its generator material identifies heat tracing, insulation, windbreaks, keeping water flowing or draining and purging lines, space heating, pre-winter checks, inventories of freeze-protection equipment, storm procedures, staffing, and plant-specific checklists. It recommends that states consider requiring winterization plans and that design account for the **lowest recorded ambient temperature for the nearest city for which historical weather data is available**, including wind-speed heat-loss effects. [1]

**OBSERVED.** Quanta’s 2012 report for the PUCT describes heat tracing, insulation, thermal enclosures, and windbreaks. It identifies documenting weather-sensitive equipment and design limits, and training affected personnel before peak seasons, as lower-cost and highly effective practices. The 2021 PUCT adoption order required implementation of the 2012 Quanta actions and correction of known acute 2020–21 winter failures. [2] [3] [7]

**OBSERVED.** SB 1133 became effective on June 17, 2011. It required analysis and reporting on emergency-operation plans, permitted the PUCT to seek an updated plan when information was inadequate, and authorized implementation rules. SB 1133 itself did not mandate a defined plant retrofit or prescribe a penalty. [4] [5]

**UNRESOLVED.** These statutory facts support a reporting-centered regime. They do not establish that a named decision-maker rejected heat tracing, insulation, windbreaks, or a defined design-temperature requirement. Nor does the 2021 adoption order, by itself, prove that no earlier rule imposed a comparable control. The defensible conclusion is narrower: the cited record does not establish a pre-Uri, cross-ERCOT implementation mandate for the defined controls. [4] [5] [7]

**UNRESOLVED — source conflict.** A news report quotes a former PUCT chair as saying a mandate was removed before passage. The retrieved official introduced bill text, enrolled text, and amendment material do not verify that claim. It is preserved as an unverified account and is not used to satisfy Gate 2. [4] [12] [13]

**UNRESOLVED — source-version boundary.** The official sources describe different versions and are not interchangeable. The 2021 PUCT adoption order is the appropriate source for the historical rule change after Uri. The currently posted consolidated §25.55 is marked effective October 20, 2022 and may support only current-law claims, not the pre-Uri regulatory history. [7] [14]

## Causation and risk connection

**OBSERVED.** The final FERC/NERC report finds that freezing issues accounted for 44.2% and fuel issues for 31.4% of the report’s measure of unplanned generation-unit outages, derates, and failures to start. **DERIVED:** `44.2% + 31.4% = 75.6%` of that measure. The report also records the load shedding and customer disruption described above. [8]

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** FERC/NERC estimates that protecting four types of power-plant components from icing and freezing could have reduced ERCOT outages by 67%. This is a modelled counterfactual risk-reduction estimate. It is not an observed prevented-loss count and does not establish that a pre-Uri regulatory omission caused every outage, death, injury, or downstream dollar. [8]

**OBSERVED with limited inference.** The report describes a material gas-electric feedback: weather/freezing and power loss at midstream, wellhead, or gathering facilities contributed to gas-production decline. This supports a substantial, multi-cause risk connection for Gate 4, rather than sole causation by any one omitted control. Peer-reviewed analysis is consistent with that high-level account, but is corroborative rather than the primary basis for Gate 4 or for any monetary claim. [8] [15]

## Downstream cost and exposure categories

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL — reported VOLL category.** The Dallas Fed reports a **$4.3 billion** outage-cost estimate using a VOLL model with 2019 Texas gross domestic product, electricity-consumption, and retail-price inputs. It reports firm VOLL of $6,733/MWh, household VOLL of $117.60/MWh, 70.5 hours of load shed, and an average load shed close to 14,000 MW. [9]

**DERIVED — limited arithmetic check.** `14,000 MW × 70.5 h = 987,000 MWh`, and `$4.3 billion ÷ 987,000 MWh = $4,356.64/MWh`. The source does not provide the load allocation needed to reconstruct the blended $4.3 billion from its separate firm and household VOLL values. The correct description is therefore: **reported VOLL model; inputs are partly disclosed, and the exact total is not independently reproducible from the page.** It is neither an invoice nor an audited loss total, and it is not a valuation of deaths or injuries. [9]

**OBSERVED — gross claim/exposure.** ERCOT’s current page, still styled as a proposed Brazos settlement, states that ERCOT sought recovery of **approximately $1.9 billion** in unpaid Winter Storm Uri settlement invoices from Brazos Electric Power Cooperative. This supports a discrete wholesale-market claim/exposure category. It does not establish payment, a net loss, or a standalone realized statewide cost. [10]

**UNRESOLVED — accounting treatment.** The $4.3 billion VOLL category and the approximately $1.9 billion Brazos claim are **non-additive**. They use different accounting concepts, may overlap economically, and lack a common basis for a net total. The broader early estimates of $80 billion–$130 billion in direct and indirect economic losses and $10 billion–$20 billion in insured losses reported on the Dallas Fed page are also **non-additive** and are not used as a total. [9]

**OBSERVED.** A narrow reporting or plant-budget boundary excludes material effects that crossed to households, firms, service systems, market participants, and public institutions during Uri. The following categories remain segregated rather than summed.

**UNRESOLVED — no boundary delta.** The record cannot calculate a defensible avoided-cost or net-cost comparison. The original control cost and decision-specific rationale are not established, the 67% figure is conditional rather than an observed loss allocation, and the downstream categories are non-additive. No allocation, attribution ratio, cross-control transfer, or systemwide avoided-cost claim is made.

## Disposition, falsifier, and next evidence test

**Disposition: REJECTED / not selected.** The candidate fails the preregistered all-gates rule and must not be presented as a qualified cost-boundary proof.

**PLANNED — falsifier for this disposition.** The decision would change only if a primary, contemporaneous 2011–20 PUCT or legislative docket, ERCOT board record, generator capital-authorization record, or sworn or court-admitted evidence documents all of the following: a defined winterization control or standard; the responsible actor’s decision to decline, remove, defer, or weaken it; and that decision’s ex-ante cost, saving, schedule value, or explicit cost rationale. A subsequent analysis would retain the source currency and year and compare only clearly non-overlapping downstream categories.

**PLANNED — contrary evidence test.** The risk narrative would need revision if primary evidence showed that the relevant controls were mandatory and implemented across the affected assets before Uri, or that their absence was unrelated to later cold-weather risk. Such evidence would not, by itself, supply the missing Gate 3 cost link.

**UNRESOLVED.** The $50,000–$500,000 range requires source verification for its original calculation, scope, and whether its unit is a plant or unit. The public account of a removed mandate remains unverified in the retrieved official legislative material. The 67% component-protection estimate must remain a modelled counterfactual rather than a loss-allocation ratio. The monetary categories remain non-additive.
