# WO-3 evidence record — human, legal, financial, and institutional crossings

**Item:** WO-3 / M5
**Verdict:** **INCOMPLETE; reframe the channel as relational stocks plus events, not as a single flow/rate.**

## Conclusion

The preregistered M5 category names labor, maintenance, ownership, legal duties, liability, funding, and accounting claims. That is a useful prompt list, but it is not an operationally complete medium. Its formal quantities are cumulative inbound and outbound amounts over a horizon, divided by time. A legal permission, veto, guarantee, lien, fiduciary power, regulatory condition, or voting right can already exist at the beginning of the horizon and remain unchanged through it. It nonetheless **constrains, enables, burdens, or exposes** an outside party—the metric's definition of a crossing—while generating no M5 event that the stated `Qm,in/out` and `Rm` rules unambiguously count.

The decisive counterexample is a system with an already-issued external guarantee or operating licence at `t0`, no payment, amendment, inspection, message, or labor during finite `H`, and an external holder or regulator. The guarantee gives its holder a call right and can impose a present stand-ready obligation even when payment is remote. A licence or rule can determine what the system may do without any contemporaneous resource transfer. Accounting and administrative-law sources treat these as real present or contingent relations, not as mere future cash flows or messages. [1] [2] [3] [4] On the present protocol, `Q` might be reported as zero, or “obligation” might be entered ad hoc as a quantity; neither outcome is defined for a relation that predates `H` and is unchanged during it. The resulting rate is therefore neither a reliable absence claim nor a measure of the continuing coupling.

This record **does not establish a case-specific crossing rate**. No named system, boundary, register, contract set, or horizon was supplied. The observations below establish that the omitted relation types exist in law, accounting, and finance. The conclusion that they must be represented as crossing candidates is a **proposed amendment** derived from WO-3's own expansive definition of a crossing.

## Observed evidence

**Legal and financial obligations are not exhausted by payments.** FASB defines a liability as a present obligation to transfer an economic benefit, says obligations may require an entity to stand ready, and gives options, guarantees, and warranties as obligations even where the holder may never exercise the option or an outflow is remote. [1] The IFRS description of IAS 37 likewise distinguishes provisions from contingent liabilities: a contingent liability may be a possible obligation confirmed by future events not wholly under the entity's control, or an obligation not recognized because settlement is not probable or cannot be reliably measured. [2] The IMF states that a guarantee is a legally binding undertaking, does not require upfront budget cash outflow when issued, and creates risk of future outflows; it also describes implicit guarantees as non-contractual and non-enforceable expectations that can create material fiscal risk. [3]

**Institutional coupling includes permissions, status, and coercive authority.** The U.S. Administrative Procedure Act defines a rule as an agency statement with future effect designed to implement, interpret, or prescribe law or policy. It separately defines licence, order, sanction, relief, and agency action; a sanction can be a prohibition, requirement, limitation, penalty, or other compulsory or restrictive action. [4] These are authority relations and state changes. They need not be transfers of currency or labour. A peer-reviewed analysis of collective legal relations further identifies powers, immunities, liberties, and disabilities alongside claim-rights and duties. [5]

**Financial relation structures can couple parties before settlement.** A peer-reviewed financial-network model studies contingent payments whose nominal interbank obligations depend on firms' wealth, and treats their effects through clearing wealth and contagion. [6] This is direct support for recording contingent financial couplings separately from observed payment flows; it does not prove that any particular candidate has such a coupling.

**Measurement cannot turn non-detection into an exact universal zero.** NIST's metrology material defines measurement as an experimental process obtaining quantity values, and requires an uncertainty-bearing, documented calibration chain for metrological traceability. [7] NIST's discussion of the International Vocabulary of Metrology says uncertainty describes a dispersion or range of values believed to contain the true measurand. [8] These sources support the limited empirical statement “not detected under a stated method and bound,” rather than a general inference from no detection to exact absence.

> “Guarantees do not involve any upfront cash outflow from the budget at the time of issuance but they expose the government to the risk of future cash outflows.” — IMF Fiscal Affairs Department [3]

## Proposed operational amendment

For this family, use a **relational boundary** in addition to the system boundary. Let `P_S` be the legal persons, accounts, roles, and governing bodies counted as `S`; let `P_O` be parties outside it; and declare the applicable jurisdictions, registries, and financial-reporting perimeter. A human/legal/financial/institutional relation crosses the boundary when it allocates a right, duty, power, immunity, authority, claim, exposure, or status between `P_S` and `P_O`, or when an outside institution can change `S`'s permitted action set.

Retain event flows, but add a status vector. For a relation type `r`, record `A_r(B,t)`, the number of active cross-boundary relations at time `t`, and `T_r(B,H)`, the count of creations, amendments, exercises, settlements, suspensions, and terminations during `H`. Report the time integral `∫_H A_r(B,t)dt` in relation-days, rather than forcing every relation into currency per day or transactions per day. Financial subchannels may additionally report contractual maximum exposure and model-dependent expected exposure. A positive, source-supported active relation should establish `L > 0` for the terminality test even where no cash, labor, or message is observed during `H`.

This is a **proposed measurement design**, not an observed result. It is intentionally non-additive: one board appointment could produce legal, institutional, human, and financial consequences. The protocol should identify a primary witnessing subchannel and cross-reference the rest, because the terminality test needs one positive witness, not a misleading grand total.

## Can an exact zero upper bound be empirically verified?

**For the literal WO-3 falsifier—an actual nonempty system with zero crossings in every medium for all time—the answer is no as an empirical procedure.** This is a **derived** conclusion from the metric's own definition plus measurement practice, not an empirical observation about a particular candidate. To obtain experimental evidence about `S`, an external instrument or observer must acquire a record correlated with `S`. Under the metric, a signal, observation, record, or measurement is an information crossing. If the evidence comes from a construction log, registry, inspection, or operator report, that evidence either records a prior crossing or is itself a crossing when produced. Moving the observer inside `S` does not solve the problem: communicating the result to an external verifier becomes the crossing.

A later, passive observation may be outside a deliberately short `H`; that can avoid adding a crossing **within that artificially limited interval**, but it does not validate whole-life zero, and it cannot retroactively provide error-free completeness. It can support only a bounded historical statement such as: *within the declared legal/financial inventory and finite interval, no event above specified detection/coverage limits was found.* A complete official registry can sometimes support a zero **recorded-event** count for that registry. It cannot by itself establish zero unrecorded claims, implicit expectations, unknown jurisdictions, or every possible medium.

A proof that a closed formal model has no permitted transitions is different. It may establish zero **within the model**, provided its state space, boundary, and rules are stipulated as complete. It does not empirically verify that a real system instantiates the model, that its construction was crossing-free, or that its observational evidence has not already crossed the boundary. Exact `U = 0` for a real, open-ended ontology therefore remains a model-level assumption; the defensible empirical output is a finite detection/coverage bound with unresolved channels declared.

## Disposition for WO-3

The work order's general challenge survives only in weakened form: the failure of a candidate to show a positive M5 flow is not evidence of no human, legal, financial, or institutional crossing. The current “terminal within scope” rule should not be awarded from zero M5 rates alone. It must require (1) explicit relation-stock inventory, (2) declared legal and financial perimeter, (3) coverage limits for hidden and implicit relations, and (4) a distinction between a zero **event count**, no detection above a threshold, and an exact zero upper bound. Until then, M5 is an incomplete inventory and the literal operational falsifier is not empirically attainable.
