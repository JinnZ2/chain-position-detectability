# WO-3 evidence record — Forsmark KBS-3 spent-fuel repository

**Case ID:** `WO3-FORSMARK-KBS3-2025`
**Candidate:** Sweden’s **Forsmark Spent Fuel Repository**, a KBS-3 deep geological repository project at Söderviken, Östhammar Municipality.
**Record date:** 19 September 2026
**Protocol:** WO-3 crossing-rate metric, preregistered before case research.
**Classification:** **nonterminal within the stipulated boundary and contingent declared whole-life horizon**.

## Finding

**OBSERVED — decisive basis.** Forsmark is nonterminal for the declared whole-life accounting frame because construction-stage surface works and institutional interactions have already crossed the stipulated boundary. SKB announced ground-breaking on 15 January 2025, and its project material reports completed forest clearance and ground preparation. These are observed interactions with external environmental material. They support a positive event-count lower bound for construction-stage matter crossing under the declared boundary, but do **not** provide a measured mass or volume transfer. [1] [14]

**OBSERVED — independent basis.** The 2022 Government decision, continuing Swedish Radiation Safety Authority (SSM) review, required future safety reports, annual stakeholder meetings, and a later Government decision on permanent sealing establish existing information, control, and human/institutional interactions across the same boundary. These actual interfaces independently reject terminality; they are not merely prospective publicity. [3] [4]

The classification is an **accounting result, not a safety finding**. It does not assert a present radionuclide release, a failure of containment, or non-compliance with a model-conditional safety case or regulatory risk criterion. Major underground excavation, emplacement, post-emplacement heat transfer, and radionuclide transport remain planned, conditional, or unresolved as specified below.

## Scope, stipulated boundary, and horizon

### System `S`

`S` is the engineered and governed Forsmark repository artifact: surface and underground repository works, access ramp, excavated openings, emplacement equipment, fuel canisters, bentonite buffer, backfill, plugs, closure works, repository-specific records and monitoring, and the operator’s repository-specific obligations. The candidate is a licensed project rather than a generic repository abstraction. **PLANNED / SCHEDULED:** the reference project is intended to be approximately 500 m deep, accommodate about 12,000 tonnes of spent fuel in 6,000 canisters, and ultimately contain more than 60 km of tunnels. [1] [2]

### Boundary `B`

`B` is a **stipulated accounting boundary**, not an independently observed physical fact. It separates `S` from the pre-existing Forsmark bedrock and groundwater; surface air, water, soils, ecosystems, and climate; workers and residents; suppliers and transport networks; the Government, SSM, courts, and municipalities; and recipients of records or measurements. The engineered barriers are inside `S`; the natural host-rock barrier is external.

This choice makes construction-stage interaction with external environmental material, any post-emplacement heat transfer to host rock, and oversight or communications eligible crossings. Treating host rock, groundwater, regulators, workers, or record recipients as internal would define a different measurand and could suppress these channels. The boundary does not claim that external environmental material has been weighed or that any particular external person is currently radiologically exposed.

### Horizon `H`

The declared whole-life horizon is:

> `H = [15 January 2025, closure + 1,000,000 years]`.

This is a **contingent, declared horizon**. It begins with observed construction and extends through construction, operation, sealing, and afterlife. Its end date and duration are not numerically fixed because permanent sealing requires a future Government decision and no closure date is established in this record. The Government described construction and operation until the fuel is stored as an approximately 70-year project; that estimate is not a closure date. [4]

`Hreg = [closure, closure + approximately 100,000 years]` is retained only as a **protocol regulatory quantitative sub-horizon**, not as a statutory terminality endpoint. The legally operative reference is SSM’s consolidated Swedish version of SSMFS 2008:37, including the 2018:19 amendment. The English translation is retained only as an informational aid because it expressly lacks legal force. The regulation and its general advice call for detailed analysis in the first 1,000 years, quantitative risk analysis to approximately 100,000 years or a glaciation cycle, and extension toward one million years when informative. Beyond approximately 100,000 years, strict numerical comparison with the individual-risk criterion is not meaningful; barrier performance, radionuclide fluxes, and environmental concentrations are instead relevant indicators. These are risk-assessment instructions, not a zero-crossing certification. [5] [6]

## Construction status and the excavation conflict

**OBSERVED.** The decisive construction witness is deliberately narrow. Ground-breaking, forest clearance, and ground preparation show that preparatory surface works occurred. Within the stipulated `B`, those works interacted with external environmental material, so the evidence supports a positive event-count lower bound. It does not establish an observed rock-removal volume, an underground-excavation event, or a particular construction mass balance. [1] [14]

**PLANNED / SCHEDULED.** Major excavation must not be backdated into the observed witness. SKB’s licensing page stated that the repository safety analysis was under SSM review and that rock excavation could not start until SSM approval. Its project page reports around 2.7 million m³ of rock over several decades, whereas SKB International reports approximately 2.3 million m³ over several decades. Both are first-party estimates, and neither is treated as definitive, cumulative, or already excavated. [1] [2] [3]

**UNRESOLVED.** No current cumulative excavated volume or mass was located. No annual excavation rate is reported. In particular, this record makes no `230,000 m³/year` claim: the cited ten-year period concerns the point before disposal may begin, while excavation and tunnel development are described as continuing for several decades and in parallel with disposal. [1] [2]

No whole-horizon average is reported. A quotient such as `2.3 × 10^6 / 1,000,070 = 2.29984 m³/year` would be conditional on both selecting the lower conflicting estimate and assuming a 70-year interval from January 2025 to closure. Neither condition is established. Selecting 2.7 million m³ would produce a different conditional quotient. Such arithmetic would not be an observed rate and is omitted from the result.

## Safety-case and regulatory evidence

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** The 2011 SR-Site safety assessment, whose publication record states that it was updated on 15 May 2015, models release, groundwater transport, biosphere transfer, and dose under repository and scenario assumptions. It does not observe a current Forsmark leak. The reference design’s thermal constraints and the transport models remain useful evidence of what the safety case evaluates, but they cannot provide an unconditional post-closure release probability or present flux. [7] [8] [12]

**PLANNED / SCHEDULED.** SKB’s 2023 page lists updated post-closure analyses TR-21-01 through TR-21-07 for the preliminary safety analysis report (PSAR) version. Those analyses should not be read as proof that every construction stage had already obtained the relevant SSM approval; current stage-specific review status is recorded separately in the licensing material. [3] [13]

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** The regulation’s post-closure individual-risk criterion of `10^-6` per year for a representative person in the most exposed group is a safety-performance criterion. It cannot be converted into a finding that all matter, energy, information, institutional, or dependency crossings are zero. [5] [6]

## Dependencies and unresolved quantities

**OBSERVED.** The licence and review arrangements show that external institutions exchange approvals, reports, decisions, and responsibility-relevant records with the repository system. They are a crossing witness under the stipulated boundary. They do not establish an actual radiological-exposure dependent, and this record does not rely on `D > 0` for that purpose. [3] [4]

**MODELLED / CONDITIONAL.** Potential post-closure transport and exposure depend on the actual inventory, cooling time, canister condition, buffer and backfill performance, fracture representation, groundwater chemistry and flow, climate and glaciation, and future biosphere conditions. The model literature retains those dependencies rather than resolving them to zero. [8] [9] [10] [11]

**UNRESOLVED.** This record does not establish current cumulative rock removal; construction electricity, fuel, or water balance; supply-chain mass; worker-hours; the number of data records or observations; an actual heat-output time series; a failed-canister probability distribution applicable to the final configuration; post-closure radionuclide release; biological transfer quantities; or the duration and effectiveness of records and institutional continuity. Non-observation of a value does not make its upper bound zero.

## Exact-zero ontology and the falsifier

The metric’s literal terminal-within-scope result requires a complete enumerated channel inventory with zero upper bounds for all channels over the stated boundary and horizon. A measurement communicated from `S` to an external observer is itself an information crossing. Accordingly, observation and reporting normally create an empirical-certification problem for literal zero unless the observer and records are included inside `B`.

This point is deliberately narrow. It does **not** show that finite-scope empirical testing cannot yield useful detection limits, and it does not show that every formal model proof is a physical proof. A finite-horizon finding may state that no crossing was detected above specified bounds. A formal model may demonstrate zero crossings only conditional on its boundary, channel inventory, assumptions, and mathematics. Neither statement establishes that every possible physical crossing has an upper bound of exactly zero for all time.

## Asymptote status and conclusion

Forsmark remains an **asymptote candidate classified nonterminal only for this stipulated `B` and contingent `H`**. The validated support for that classification is **OBSERVED** preparatory surface construction and **OBSERVED** institutional/information interfaces. The planned excavation totals, conditional heat inference, and modelled radionuclide routes are corroborating context, not decisive proof. The classification makes no claim about other deep geological repositories, Voyager, black holes, or any candidate assessed under another boundary or horizon.

The terminal falsifier is not met for this case. A construction-stage positive witness and independently observed institutional crossings are sufficient under the preregistered classification rule. The conclusion neither predicts a leak nor judges the repository unsafe.
