# Validation — `flint-water-corrosion-control`

**Verdict: NEEDS CORRECTION; not publication-ready as written.** The retained conclusion is sound: **Flint does not qualify as a WO-3 cost-boundary case on the reviewed record.** Gates 1, 2, 4, and 5 have adequate support; gate 3 fails because no contemporaneous, control-specific cost, saving, schedule value, or cost rationale was established for the decision to defer corrosion-control treatment (CCT). This is a failure of the qualification rule, not proof that no such record exists anywhere.

## Decision audit

| Preregistered gate | Validation | Basis and required wording |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Specific engineering control available | **Pass** | Orthophosphate/CCT was a specific, known control. The federal court record quotes a March 26, 2014 email anticipating at least 1 mg/L total phosphorus because phosphate would be added for distribution-system corrosion control. The peer-reviewed case study independently describes the prior orthophosphate dose and the post-switch absence of phosphate. [3] [4] |
| 2. Control declined, removed, deferred, or weakened | **Pass** | The task force reports that MDEQ allowed two six-month monitoring periods before deciding whether CCT was necessary. EPA OIG says MDEQ advised additional tests and delayed CCT installation. Describe this as a **regulator-directed delay/non-requirement**, rather than an established municipal cost-cutting reversal. [1] [2] |
| 3. Contemporaneous, control-specific ex-ante cost/saving/schedule/rationale | **Fail — dispositive** | None of the inspected primary/investigative records supplies a dated control price, avoided cost, schedule value, or cost rationale connected to deferring orthophosphate/CCT. The court describes the 2011 reference as a “placeholder for a possible corrosion control inhibitor” in a cost analysis, not a decision-linked CCT price. [3] |
| 4. Authoritative omitted-control-to-later-harm/risk connection | **Pass, qualified** | The task force says MDEQ’s interpretation led to not requiring CCT; EPA OIG says the delay prolonged lead exposure. The peer-reviewed case study documents the untreated switch, deterioration of protective scales, and high lead release. The technical review cautions that the water may have remained sufficiently corrosive to exceed the action level even with phosphate. These sources establish a material risk/harm connection, **not** but-for proof that CCT would have prevented every injury or every response expense. [1] [2] [4] [5] |
| 5. Supported downstream-cost category | **Pass only as a category, not causal apportionment** | EPA OIG reports MDEQ’s statement that more than $400 million in public funds had been spent by EPA and Michigan to address the crisis. Michigan currently reports a live total of $448,241,284 “spent to date.” Each is an aggregate response/recovery figure. Neither allocates a dollar to CCT omission, and they must not be summed or treated as a counterfactual cost. [2] [6] |

## Corrections required before publication

1. **Delete or correct the `$4 million reconnection figure` and its citation.** The cited task-force report does not reproduce that figure in the accessible report text. It instead records a March 2015 statement that return to DWSD would cost **$12 million**, and elsewhere reports an asserted **$10.1 million per year** reconnection cost plus purchases up to **$1 million per month**. These are return-to-Detroit/source-reversal statements, not CCT costs. [1] The `$4 million` attribution therefore fails citation verification.

2. Retain the `$171,000` exclusion, but describe it precisely. The federal court record says Flint retained LAN for professional engineering assistance to operate the treatment plant on Flint River water for about two years and later convert to KWA water, “at a cost of $171,000.00”; the court says this reflected fees for two proposed tasks. It is not a price for orthophosphate, nor evidence of a saving from omitting CCT. [3]

3. The assertion that a later court decision found the 2014 plan ambiguous because no permit or physical orthophosphate feed system existed is **not validated by the supplied citation**: the cited Casemine page now returns only a login page. Do not publish that factual qualification from this citation. Either cite the filed order/underlying docket material directly or omit it. The accessible February 2022 court opinion supports the March 26 plan and the MDEQ non-mandate, but not that added permit/feed-system formulation. [3]

4. Preserve the rejection of the often repeated `$100–$150/day` claim. It was not substantiated in the reviewed contemporaneous decision material. Do not replace it with a retrospective estimate, general source-switch savings, reconnection costs, plant-upgrade costs, or the `$171,000` engineering-services fee. Those are cross-control cost transfers barred by gate 3.

5. Replace “**falsified as a qualified cost case if such a document cannot be produced**” with: “**does not qualify on the current reviewed record; it could qualify only if a contemporaneous, decision-linked CCT cost/rationale record is produced.**” A bounded search failure is not evidence that the record never existed. This is an evidentiary-status correction, not a relaxation of the preregistered gate.

6. Tighten the frame before any cost calculation. The stated horizon begins at “March 2014 planning” and ends at unspecified “later documented response and recovery expenditures.” It is not a closed interval and cannot support a present-value comparison. State an exact decision date, a fixed cut-off/access date, price basis, included payer categories, and a non-overlap rule. No valid delta can currently be calculated.

## Arithmetic and overlap check

**No decision-versus-downstream delta is reported, and none should be inferred.** The record correctly declines arithmetic because the gate-3 numerator/control cost is absent. The following figures are not additive: (i) OIG’s “more than $400 million” historical public-funds statement, (ii) the current Michigan expenditure dashboard’s `$448,241,284` rolling total, (iii) the `$100 million` March 2017 EPA infrastructure award, and (iv) its required `$20 million` state match. The award and match may be components of broader response spending; the live dashboard has an unstated current cut-off and potentially different accounting scope. [2] [6] [7]

Michigan’s response page separately says the state provided more than `$350 million` and the federal government `$100 million` for a list of recovery purposes. That is a program-level description, not a total that can be combined with the dashboard or OIG figure. [7] The settlement category is real—Michigan says the court gave final approval to a partial settlement on March 3, 2022—but the official landing page does not state a total. It must remain a category only. [8] [9]

## Current-source conflict and version control

The official sources do not establish a single comparable expenditure total. The 2018 OIG report reports **more than `$400 million`** based on MDEQ information, while Michigan’s live dashboard displayed **`$448,241,284 spent to date`** when checked on **2026-09-19**. These values differ in date, apparent source/payer scope, and accounting basis; they are not competing estimates that can be silently substituted. Record the retrieval date and dashboard value on every reuse. [2] [6]

The current Michigan response page continues to state “more than `$350 million`” state plus `$100 million` federal and currently says Flint has met lead standards for 15 consecutive monitoring periods, with a latest 90th-percentile value of 3 ppb. These are current-page statements, not replacements for the historical 2014–2017 evidence. EPA’s current FAQ likewise describes present Flint/GLWA phosphate arrangements; it must not be used to backfill the 2014 decision record. [7] [10]

## Ontology and asymptote check

This is a **cost-case evidence record**, not an asymptote assessment. It contains no claim of a zero crossing, no modelled/theoretical crossing claim, and no crossing-rate calculation. The requested test for “at least one positive crossing after correction” is therefore **not applicable** to this item; it must not be marked passed by implication.

Nor can observed response expenditures, a regulatory deferral, or measurement of later lead exposure certify the work order’s universal ontology claim. They are evidence of specified historical events inside the stated system boundary. They do not empirically prove every possible crossing is nonzero, nor do they supply model-conditional proof of that proposition. The record should retain the narrower inference: aggregate downstream costs demonstrate that an expanded accounting boundary contains real categories of burden, while their causal allocation to the omitted control remains unmeasured.
