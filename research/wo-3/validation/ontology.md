# WO-3 ontology validation — terminal-system accounting boundary

**Item:** `ontology`
**Validation date:** 19 September 2026
**Verdict:** **Needs correction; not publication-ready.**

## Retained finding

The record’s defensible core is retained. **No selected asymptote is terminal in its declared frame once a positive witness is correctly labelled**, and the cost screen correctly returns a bounded null: none of the six records clears all five preregistered gates. The ontology audits also correctly show that a six-row, scalar-rate scheme is not adequate for an all-medium zero claim. The strongest general result is narrower than the draft states: **finite empirical work can establish detections or scope-qualified non-detections and upper bounds; it cannot empirically certify exact all-medium, all-time zero for a real system. A zero result can instead be proved only conditionally within a stipulated model, boundary, inventory, and premises.** [1] [2]

## Boundary and evidence-status disposition

The approved boundary discipline is retained, with two changes. First, a **positive observed crossing** may reject terminality for its declared boundary and horizon, but a finite non-detection only supports a detection-limited result. Second, a model can prove exact zero only after it stipulates a closed inventory, physical laws, boundary, initial conditions, and parameter values. That is a **model-conditional proof**, not empirical certification that a real system has those properties. The distinction must appear next to every exact-zero claim, not only in a methods section. [1] [2]

The asymptote correction does not reverse any nonterminal decision. Forsmark has an observed construction witness regardless of unresolved post-closure release modelling. Voyager retains a **conditional modelled** thermal witness after radio cessation, not an observed 2035 witness. GW150914 retains an observed external waveform and a **model-inferred** source-energy witness, not a direct measurement at the invented one-light-year boundary. Hawking radiation remains theoretical. [3] [5] [8] [10]

## Source conflicts and version control

The decisive current-source conflict is SKB’s planned excavation volume: **about 2.7 million m³** on the principal project page reviewed 17 June 2025 versus **about 2.3 million m³** on SKB International’s page reviewed 25 March 2026. The two pages agree about the project’s construction status and broad configuration, but the validation record must not silently choose either number. [3] [4]

NASA’s current operations update of 17 April 2026 also changes the evidentiary context from older planning materials: Voyager 1 remained active, had two operating science instruments, and was still sending data after an instrument shutdown. It does not contradict the 2035 illustrative model, but confirms that permanent radio cessation remains a proposed future condition rather than an observation. [6]

## Publication disposition

Publication is appropriate only after the synthesis changes the status labels and wording above, corrects the Forsmark citation/volume conflict, removes the unsupported ten-year excavation-rate implication, and states the empirical-versus-model distinction in its decision paragraph and asymptote table. **Until then, publication-ready status is false.**
