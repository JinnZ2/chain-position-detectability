# Validation — WO-3-BH-GW150914 (black-hole)

**Verdict:** **needs correction**. **Publication-ready:** **No**, until the finite-horizon energy/rate claim and the observed-versus-derived wording are corrected. The retained conclusion is narrower: **GW150914 is nonterminal for the declared enclosing source-system frame conditional on the gravitational-wave source attribution and propagation model.** A statistically significant two-detector signal was observed; a general-relativistic waveform analysis derives its source association and its radiated energy. This is not a direct observation of either event-horizon interior or of a one-light-year crossing surface.[2] [3]

## 1. Decision-critical audit

1. **Candidate, boundary, and whole-life scope.** It is legitimate to assess the inferred GW150914 binary/remnant system rather than an event-horizon interior alone. A one-light-year source-frame world-tube is a conventional accounting boundary, not a physical surface, and must remain explicitly model/coordinate dependent. Its use is appropriate only after correcting every statement that treats an outward crossing of that unobserved tube as directly observed. The wider horizon beginning at black-hole formation and having an open endpoint is also appropriate for the whole-life question. A later positive witness is sufficient to reject whole-life terminality under the preregistered rule; the record need not reconstruct formation to do so.[1]

2. **Positive crossing survives, but not as currently quantified.** LIGO Hanford and Livingston observed a coincident transient on 14 September 2015. The original discovery analysis reports matched-filter SNR 24, false-alarm rate below one per 203,000 years, significance above 5.1 sigma, frequency sweep 35–250 Hz, peak strain \(1.0\times10^{-21}\), and a waveform matching a binary-black-hole inspiral, merger, and ringdown.[2] The official open-data page independently supplies the detector data and confirms the false-alarm/significance result.[3]

   Thus at least one positive crossing **remains after correction**: conditional on the supported general-relativistic source attribution, an outgoing gravitational-wave signal propagated from the inferred source through every enclosing exterior surface, including \(B_{1\,ly}\). This is a **derived physical crossing**, supported by an observed detector signal—not an observed crossing of \(B_{1\,ly}\). It is enough for a model-conditional nonterminal classification. It does not empirically certify a literal universal proposition about all channels or directly observe the interior.

3. **The stated \(Q\) and \(R\) do not match \(H_{cross}\).** The paper’s \(3.0^{+0.5}_{-0.5}M_\odot c^2\) is a source-model estimate of radiated energy; it is not cited as energy emitted solely in the interval \([t_m-10\,s,t_m+10\,s]\). The declared surface-crossing window can include radiation emitted in that source-time interval, one light-year later, but the record provides no primary-source allocation showing that its full 2.5-\(M_\odot c^2\) posterior endpoint crossed within that window. The record therefore cannot report \(Q_{out}(B_{1\,ly},H_{cross})\geq_{90\%}4.47\times10^{47}\) J or its derived mean rate. This is a scope/horizon error, not an arithmetic error.

   Correct either by (a) changing the quantitative horizon to include every emission epoch represented by the published radiated-energy estimate, leaving its duration and average rate unreported if it is not specified; or (b) retaining the finite window but replacing the total-energy/rate figures with a primary-source-supported energy integral confined to that window. Until then, retain a positive **event** witness only, with no joule or watt lower bound for \(H_{cross}\).

4. **Evidence-status labels need tightening.** “Observed strain” is acceptable only as an instrument-level measurement subject to calibration. “Observed external waveform,” “observed outgoing burst crosses the declared outer boundary,” and “recovered source waveform” overstate what was observed. Replace them with: **OBSERVED: calibrated detector strain data and coincident transient; DERIVED: gravitational-wave interpretation, source parameters, source-to-exterior association, one-light-year crossing, and radiated energy, conditional on the LIGO/Virgo waveform and cosmological models.** The source paper itself states that the source parameters come from waveform modelling and quotes 90% credible intervals.[2]

5. **Ontology check.** Measurement establishes a detector-level interaction/information record; it does not by itself prove that the source system, as bounded one light-year from an inferred barycentre, had a particular crossing. The causal/source claim follows only with the physical model. Therefore observation can empirically certify a signal at the detector, while a zero-crossing claim for a remote source remains model conditional. The record’s human/institutional row is also misclassified: data recording, release, funding, and scientific work occurred **outside** \(B_{1\,ly}\); they are downstream consequences/evidence, not themselves a human or institutional transfer across that source boundary. Remove that row as a metric crossing or label it “external observational context; not counted.” The dependency row may remain **derived and unquantified**, but must not assert a source-to-detector causal link as an observation.

6. **Classical horizon and Hawking claims.** The narrow classical proposition is sound when stated conditionally: an ideal classical event horizon is one-way, so an outward signal launched from its interior is not predicted to emerge. It cannot establish zero **inbound** crossings or zero crossings of the binary/remnant-plus-exterior system. Hawking’s 1975 result is a semiclassical prediction of particle emission, mass loss, and eventual disappearance, while Page’s rotating-hole calculation shows that emission and lifetime depend on spin.[5] [6] The record correctly excludes Hawking radiation as an observed GW150914 flux. Retain “theoretical/semiclassical,” never “observed.” The generic accretion-disk material/radiation discussion is properly labelled non-source-specific; it cannot create a positive GW150914 accretion witness.[7]

7. **Cost gates.** This is an asymptote record, not a cost case. Gates 1–5 all fail/not established: no available engineering control, decision-maker’s decline, contemporaneous cost rationale, authoritative omitted-control-to-harm link, or downstream cost category is identified. No cost-boundary conclusion, transferred cost, aggregate total, or but-for causation may be imported from this black-hole record. This is a valid null result under the preregistered selection rule, not a basis for folklore cost claims.[1]

## 2. Calculation and citation checks

The arithmetic is reproducible **given the draft’s inputs**, but its application to the stated finite horizon is rejected as noted above. With \(M_\odot=1.98847\times10^{30}\) kg and exact \(c=299,792,458\) m/s, \(2.5M_\odot c^2=4.467869275657\times10^{47}\) J. Dividing by one Julian year plus 20 seconds (31,557,620 s) gives \(1.415781442218\times10^{40}\) W, which rounds to the draft’s \(4.47\times10^{47}\) J and \(1.416\times10^{40}\) W. The stated idealized Schwarzschild calculations also reproduce: \(P_H(66M_\odot)=2.0679\times10^{-32}\) W, \(\tau_H(59.2M_\odot)=4.3480\times10^{72}\) Julian years, and \(T_H(59.2M_\odot)=1.0422\times10^{-9}\) K. They must remain illustrative theory calculations, not metric lower bounds.[8] [9]

The original discovery paper supports the draft’s original values \(36^{+5}_{-4}\), \(29^{+4}_{-4}\), \(62^{+4}_{-4}\,M_\odot\), and \(3.0^{+0.5}_{-0.5}M_\odot c^2\), with 90% credible intervals.[2] The cited current GWOSC page instead says its parameter table was updated from a later analysis and reports \(36.2^{+5.2}_{-3.8}\), \(29.1^{+3.7}_{-4.4}\), \(62.3^{+3.7}_{-3.1}\,M_\odot\), spin \(0.68^{+0.05}_{-0.06}\), and the same radiated-energy central estimate/interval.[3] It also records a 15 May 2019 correction of the radiated-energy uncertainty to ±0.5. The draft silently combines the original-paper masses with the updated GWOSC spin/mass envelope; disclose the version difference and identify each value’s source.

The constants citation requires a date/version note. NIST currently serves the **2022 CODATA** set (web version 9.0, updated 9 May 2024), whereas the draft names no edition. Its \(G=6.67430(15)\times10^{-11}\), exact \(\hbar=1.054571817\ldots\times10^{-34}\) J s, and exact \(c\) agree with the currently served values.[8] [9] [12] [13] The adopted kilogram solar mass is a conventional numerical estimate, not an IAU-defined exact nominal solar mass: IAU 2015 makes \((GM)_\odot^N\), rather than \(M_\odot\) in kg, the exact nominal conversion constant. State the mass convention and applicable \(G\) edition.[10]

The references for Hawking and the CMB are substantively accurate, but the draft should cite the original Hawking article directly rather than an INSPIRE record, and the original Fixsen article/DOI rather than the ADS abstract page. The Finkelstein reference is historically appropriate, but the exact one-way wording should be paired with a directly accessible modern review or stated as the classical-model proposition rather than as an observation.[4] [5] [11]

## 3. Required replacement decision

**Decision after correction:** `nonterminal — model-conditional physical classification`. The empirical certification is limited to a nonzero, highly significant detector signal. The evidence supports an outgoing source-system crossing only through the declared general-relativistic inference and propagation model. Delete the finite-window joule/rate lower bound unless its horizon is expanded or a window-limited energy calculation is sourced. Do not describe the conclusion as a model-free observed source-boundary crossing, and do not use the measurement relation to claim more than empirical detector-level certification.
