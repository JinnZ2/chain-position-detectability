# Validation — Forsmark KBS-3 geological-repository candidate

**Item:** `WO3-FORSMARK-KBS3-2025`
**Validation date:** 19 September 2026
**Verdict:** **needs correction; not publication-ready.**

## Retained result

The candidate remains **nonterminal for the declared whole-life frame**, but only on a narrower and better-supported basis. SKB's construction announcement dates the ground-breaking to 15 January 2025, and its project page says forest clearance and ground preparation had been completed. Those are observed construction-stage interactions with the external environment. The 2022 Government decision and the continuing statutory review also establish actual information, control, and institutional interactions across the record's chosen boundary. Thus, even after removing all prospective radionuclide-release claims and the disputed excavation total from the decisive basis, at least one positive crossing remains. This is sufficient under the preregistered rule; no assertion of a present radionuclide leak is needed. [1] [3] [4] [14]

This result is **an accounting classification, not a safety finding**. It does not contradict a model-conditional safety case or a regulatory conclusion that the design can meet a risk criterion.

## Required corrections

1. **Separate observed surface works from unverified underground excavation.** The evidence supports ground-breaking, forest clearance, ground preparation, and preparatory above-ground works. It does not establish that the planned 2.3 or 2.7 million m³ has been excavated, nor that underground rock excavation had begun. SKB's licensing page, last reviewed 13 August 2025, says that the spent-fuel repository safety analysis was under SSM review and that rock excavation could not start until SSM approval. The record must therefore replace broad wording such as “construction entails excavation and removal” with a stage-specific statement: observed preparation is the witness; major excavation is scheduled/planned. [1] [3]

2. **Disclose, rather than select between, conflicting official excavation totals.** SKB's project page, last reviewed 17 June 2025, says around **2.7 million m³** over several decades. SKB International's page, last reviewed 25 March 2026, says approximately **2.3 million m³** over several decades. Both are first-party current pages. Neither is an observed cumulative removal total. The record may report a planned range or say that current first-party estimates conflict; it must not present 2.3 million m³ alone as the definitive project figure. [1] [2]

3. **Remove the 230,000 m³/year “construction-year” rate.** Dividing 2.3 million m³ by ten is arithmetically correct but substantively invalid. The cited ten years is the time before disposal may begin, while the same sources say excavation/tunnel development continues for several decades and in parallel with disposal. It is not a ten-year excavation denominator. [1] [2]

4. **Make the million-year average explicitly contingent and do not call it an observed rate.** `2.3×10^6 / 1,000,070 = 2.29984 m³/year`, so the printed 2.30 figure is numerically correct only if one assumes a 70-year interval from the 2025 start to closure and then one million years after closure. The Government's 70 years is an estimate for construction and operation until the fuel is stored; permanent sealing is a later government decision. Since closure is not dated, the declared horizon has a variable endpoint and its duration is not yet 1,000,070 years. With SKB's alternative 2.7-million-m³ estimate, the analogous conditional quotient would be about 2.70 m³/year. Report no whole-horizon rate until the endpoint and total are specified. [1] [2] [4]

5. **Use evidence-status labels consistently.** The record defines OBSERVED, DERIVED, MODELLED / CONDITIONAL, and UNRESOLVED, but repeatedly uses an undefined “PLANNED” label. Add **PLANNED / SCHEDULED** to the convention, or describe these statements in prose. The planned capacity, canister emplacement, total excavation, future mapping, and putative post-emplacement heat flow are not observed facts at the record date.

6. **Relabel the heat entries.** TR-11-01 supports a *reference-design constraint*: maximum per-canister thermal output 1,700 W, 6 m minimum deposition-hole spacing, 40 m tunnel spacing, and buffer temperature below 100°C. It does not measure present heat at Forsmark. `6,000 × 1,700 W = 10.2 MW`, and `10.2 MW × 70 Julian years = 2.25321264×10^16 J`; both calculations are correct, but they are counterfactual **upper design ceilings**, assuming all planned canisters are present simultaneously at the maximum for 70 years. They are neither a lower bound nor a measured output. Any “heat crosses the selected boundary after emplacement” statement is a physical inference conditional on emplacement and the stated boundary, not a current OBSERVED or merely DERIVED crossing. [7]

7. **Keep release and transport claims strictly model-conditional.** SR-Site does use COMP23 for water-phase near-field radionuclide transport and FARF31/MARFA for far-field/varying-flow transport. Its corrosion scenario tabulates log-triangular release and dissolution-rate inputs. These establish model architecture and assumptions for conditional failed-canister/release scenarios. They do **not** establish a current Forsmark release, a positive current radionuclide lower bound, or an unconditional future release probability. The peer-reviewed route, retention, and glacial papers likewise model potential releases or injected radionuclides, not contamination observations. The record generally makes this distinction correctly and should retain it. [8] [9] [10] [11]

8. **Update the regulatory citation and tighten the horizon language.** The cited English SSMFS 2008:37 PDF is an original 2009 translation that expressly lacks legal force. SSM currently provides a Swedish consolidated version, including the 2018:19 amendment; it should be the legal reference, with the English version identified as an informational aid if retained. The substance quoted is broadly supported: detailed analysis for the first 1,000 years, quantitative risk analysis to approximately 100,000 years or a glaciation cycle, extension when informative up to one million years, and no meaningful strict numerical comparison with the individual-risk criterion beyond 100,000 years. However, these are risk-assessment instructions and general advice, not a terminality measurement horizon or a zero-crossing certification. The record's `Hreg` is therefore a reasonable protocol sub-horizon, not a fixed statutory endpoint. [5] [6]

9. **Narrow the dependency/exposure claims.** Existing licences, future safety-report requirements, SSM review, annual stakeholder meetings, and a future closure decision support a positive human/institutional and information/control interface across the declared boundary. They can supply the independent positive witness if the construction witness is challenged. In contrast, a regulation requiring analysis of exposure pathways, intrusion, water, ecosystems, and biota does not empirically show that a person or organism is currently exposed, nor does it by itself prove `D > 0` for an actual radiological-exposure dependent. Keep the former as observed institutional interactions and the latter as modelled/regulated pathways. [3] [4] [5]

10. **Correct two citation/status details.** TR-11-01 is an SKB 2011 safety assessment whose publication record says “Updated 2015-05”; it is not a current observation of the project. The 2023 SKB page correctly lists TR-21-01 through TR-21-07, but the record should say these are updated PSAR-version analyses and state the current review status separately, rather than imply that all construction had already received the relevant SSM approval. Reference [12] in the candidate is invoked only in the generic source-quality group, not for a substantive finding; remove it from that group or use it to support a specific statement. [3] [12] [13]

## Boundary, horizon, and ontology check

The chosen boundary is **permissible but must be presented as a stipulated accounting boundary**, not as an independently observed physical fact. Excluding natural host rock, groundwater, regulators, workers, and record recipients makes construction, heat, oversight, and communications eligible crossings. Including any of them would change the measurand. The construction witness survives the declared boundary because the observed preparatory activity involved external environmental material and external institutions; the record should not claim a measured mass transfer without a source.

The selected whole-life horizon correctly begins before construction and extends beyond disposal, so it does not erase known lifecycle crossings. It is not fully numerically fixed, however, because “closure + 1,000,000 years” depends on an unknown closure date. Classify it as a **contingent, declared horizon**. The current regulatory horizon is post-closure and risk-focused; it neither validates nor limits the broader protocol horizon. [4] [5]

The measurement-observation point must not be overclaimed. Under this metric, a measurement communicated from the repository system to an external observer is itself an information crossing. That creates an **empirical-certification problem** for literal zero crossings: observing and reporting a candidate normally introduces a channel, unless the observer and records are placed inside the boundary. It does **not** logically prove that no finite-scope empirical test can ever supply useful detection limits, and it does not prove that every model proof is a physical proof. A finite-horizon finding may say “no crossing was detected above stated bounds.” A formal model can at most show zero crossings **conditional on its boundary, channel inventory, assumptions, and mathematics**. Neither statement establishes that every possible physical crossing is zero for all time. This distinction should be inserted explicitly wherever the record discusses the falsifier.

## Cost-case gate check

This is not a cost case. No specific control was declined; no decision-maker's omission or weakening is documented; no contemporaneous cost/saving rationale is supplied; no authoritative investigation establishes but-for causation to later harm or risk; and no downstream cost category is supported. Accordingly, the candidate satisfies **none** of the five preregistered cost-case gates. No cost-boundary conclusion may be drawn from this repository record, and no planned expenditure, safety-model risk, public responsibility, or general folklore about disposal costs may be transferred from another control decision.

## Publication decision

Do **not** publish the evidence record unchanged. Publish only after the stage-specific construction correction, disclosure of the 2.3/2.7-million-m³ official conflict, removal of the invalid ten-year rate, conditional relabelling of heat/release claims, current consolidated regulatory citation, and narrowed dependency/ontology language. With those changes, the concise retained conclusion is supportable:

> **Forsmark is nonterminal within the stated whole-life accounting boundary because observed preparatory construction and existing institutional/record interactions provide positive crossings. The safety case's post-closure heat and radionuclide statements remain model-conditional and are not observations of a present release.**
