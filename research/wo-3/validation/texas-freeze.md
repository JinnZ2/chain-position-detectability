# Validation — `texas-freeze-pre-2021-winterization`

**Verdict: NEEDS CORRECTION — NOT QUALIFIED AS A WO-3 COST CASE.** **Not publication-ready** until the gate labels, causal-status labels, and cost calculation description below are changed. The record’s decisive conclusion should be retained: no located contemporaneous primary decision record ties a defined pre-Uri winterization control to a documented cost, saving, schedule value, or cost rationale. It therefore cannot be selected under the preregistered all-gates rule.

## Validation result

The primary record supports the existence of practicable controls. The 2011 FERC/NERC investigation describes heat tracing, insulation, temporary heating, windbreaks, operating checks, and training; it recommends state consideration of submitted winterization plans and design at the **lowest recorded** ambient temperature for the nearest city with available historical data, with wind effects included. [1] Quanta’s 2012 report describes heat tracing, insulation, thermal enclosures, windbreaks, and identifies documenting sensitive equipment/design limits and pre-season personnel training as lower-cost, highly effective practices. [2] [3]

Texas SB 1133, effective June 17, 2011, required analysis and reporting on emergency-operation plans, permitted an updated plan when information was inadequate, and authorized implementation rules. Its text did not mandate a defined plant retrofit or itself prescribe a penalty. [4] [5] This supports a **reporting-centered statutory regime**, but does not establish that an identified actor rejected a specified heat-tracing, insulation, windbreak, or design-temperature requirement. Nor does the 2021 adoption of a new rule prove, by itself, that no earlier rule imposed any comparable requirement. The defensible formulation is that the cited record does not establish a pre-Uri, cross-ERCOT implementation mandate for the defined controls. The 2021 PUCT order does independently show that the new rule required implementation of 2012 Quanta actions and correction of known 2020–21 acute issues. [6]

The primary 2011 FERC/NERC report contains no $50,000–$500,000 figure in a full-text check. It says only that needed fixes would in many cases not be “unduly expensive.” [1] The Dallas Fed article, published after Uri, attributes $50,000–$500,000 per Texas gas plant in 2011 dollars to FERC/NERC. [7] It is admissible as a retrospective lead but not as the contemporaneous, decision-specific cost evidence required by Gate 3. No systemwide avoided-control-cost calculation should be introduced.

FERC/NERC’s final 2021 findings support a material multi-cause risk connection: freezing issues (44.2%) plus fuel issues (31.4%) account for 75.6% of the unplanned unit-outage/derate/failure-to-start measure. The 67% ERCOT figure is a **modelled counterfactual risk-reduction estimate** for protecting four component types, not an observed prevented-loss count and not proof that a regulatory omission caused every outage or downstream dollar. [8] Thus it supports Gate 4 as an authoritative risk connection, but the record must replace its unqualified **OBSERVED** label with **OBSERVED causes / MODELLED counterfactual reduction**.

The Dallas Fed’s $4.3 billion is a **modelled VOLL category**, not an invoice, audited loss, or observed total. It uses 70.5 hours and an average load shed close to 14,000 MW, alongside separate firm and household VOLL values. [7] ERCOT’s current Brazos page says it **sought** approximately $1.9 billion in unpaid Uri settlement invoices as part of a page still styled “Proposed” settlement; it is a gross claim/exposure, not evidence that $1.9 billion was paid or a standalone realized statewide loss. [9] These are not additive and should remain segregated.

## Strict preregistered-gate check

| Gate | Independent finding | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Defined available control | Physical and operating winterization measures are specifically documented. [1] [2] | **PASS — OBSERVED** |
| 2. Identified actor declined, removed, deferred, or weakened that control | SB 1133’s reporting design is observed, but the cited primary record does not document an actor declining a defined control. Absence of a cited mandate is not a documented decision to decline it. | **FAIL — MISSING** |
| 3. Contemporaneous ex-ante control cost/saving/schedule/rationale | The fiscal note covers PUCT reporting work, not generator controls; the 2011 report is qualitative only. [1] [10] | **FAIL — MISSING** |
| 4. Authoritative connection to later harm or risk | Final FERC/NERC findings connect freezing/fuel failures to outage risk, with the 67% reduction expressly treated here as modelled. [8] | **PASS — MIXED OBSERVED/MODELLED** |
| 5. Supported downstream cost category | Dallas Fed supplies a disclosed VOLL-based economic-loss category; it is modelled. [7] | **PASS — DERIVED/MODELLED** |

Because Gates 2 and 3 fail, the candidate is **rejected for selection**. Gate 5 and the Brazos claim cannot repair a missing decision-specific ex-ante link. The record correctly avoids unsupported but-for causation, cross-control transfer, and adding potentially overlapping totals; retain those safeguards.

## Boundary, horizon, ontology, and crossing check

The declared February 2011–February 2021 decision horizon is coherent for the regulatory narrative. Extending to the 2022 Brazos bankruptcy process is acceptable only to document a later financial-exposure category, not to imply that it occurred within the decision horizon or is a comparable cost. The decision/regulatory boundary is also **not** a whole-life physical-system boundary, so this record cannot certify terminality or nonterminality for every lifecycle phase.

This is **not an asymptote**, so an asymptote test is inapplicable. Nevertheless, at least one positive crossing remains after the monetary corrections: FERC reports 20,000 MW of rolling blackouts and more than 4.5 million Texans losing power. Under the declared boundary, this supports a positive external dependency/exposure witness, `LD > 0`, during Uri. [8] The finding is sufficient to reject terminality **for that stated frame** under the preregistered metric; it is not empirical certification of a complete channel inventory, a zero-crossing proof, or a model-independent statement about the whole lifecycle. The evidence record itself does not make the invalid claim that observing a system proves all its crossings are zero.

## Calculation and citation corrections

1. **Retain** `44.2% + 31.4% = 75.6%`. Label the component-protection `67%` as a modelled counterfactual, not observation. [8]
2. `14,000 MW × 70.5 h = 987,000 MWh`. `US$4.3 billion / 987,000 MWh = US$4,356.64/MWh`. The Dallas Fed page supplies firm VOLL of $6,733/MWh and household VOLL of $117.60/MWh but not the load allocation needed to reproduce its blended $4.3 billion exactly. Replace “separately reproducible” with **“reported VOLL model; inputs are partly disclosed, exact total not independently reproducible from the page.”** [7]
3. Replace every formulation that treats the Brazos `approximately $1.9 billion` as a settlement cost with **“ERCOT sought recovery of approximately $1.9 billion in unpaid settlement invoices.”** Do not infer payment, net loss, or a total that may be added to VOLL. [9]
4. Amend the statutory description to “SB 1133 itself did not mandate a defined retrofit or prescribe a penalty,” rather than implying it disproves penalties available under other authority. [4]
5. Update the 2011 recommendation wording from “lowest historically recorded ambient temperature” to the source’s “lowest recorded ambient temperature for the nearest city for which historical weather data is available,” with wind-speed heat loss. [1]
6. Add the underlying final FERC/NERC report to the citation for the 2021 causal percentages; the current draft cites only FERC’s announcement. [8]

## Official-source date/version note

There is no contradiction in the core historical sequence, but the official sources are different versions and must not be silently interchanged. SB 1133’s official action history records effectiveness on June 17, 2011. [5] PUCT Project 51840 was adopted October 21, 2021 and published as the historical adoption order. [6] The currently posted consolidated §25.55 is a later version, marked effective October 20, 2022, with more detailed annual winter-preparation requirements. [11] Cite the 2021 order for the before-Uri historical change and the 2022-effective text only for current-law claims. Likewise, ERCOT’s current Brazos page still labels the agreement “Proposed”; it supports a **sought** amount, not a completed-payment claim. [9]
