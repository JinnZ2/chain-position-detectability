# WO-3 challenger validation record

**Item:** `challenger-o-ring-launch-constraint`
**Validator disposition:** **NEEDS CORRECTION — not publication-ready as written.**
**Retained result:** **Do not select Challenger as a qualifying WO-3 cost case.** The record correctly finds that it fails preregistered Gate 3, despite strong evidence for an available launch no-go control, its reversal, the accident mechanism, and documented post-accident budget categories.

## Decision

The candidate remains a **partial** case, not a qualifying case. The Rogers Commission establishes that Thiokol engineering recommended temperatures at or above 53 °F and initially recommended against the scheduled launch; after a caucus, Thiokol management recommended proceeding and no dissent was voiced on the loop. The Commission called the decision flawed and found it highly unlikely that the relevant decision-makers would have approved the launch had they known the material facts. [1] The Commission separately found that the right-SRM aft-field-joint combustion-gas leak initiated at or shortly after ignition and led to breakup and loss of *Challenger*. [2]

Those facts support Gates 1 and 2. They also satisfy Gate 4 **only in its protocol wording—connection to later harm or risk**. They do not prove the stronger but-for statement that a temperature no-go, a scrub, or a different launch date would certainly have prevented the loss. The surviving counterfactual is the Commission's qualified finding about approval on January 28, not an all-world causal proof. The House investigation supports the broader pre-accident redesign history, including its finding that schedule and cost had been prioritized over safety, but that retrospective program-history finding is not a contemporaneous rationale for the January 27 reversal. [3]

Gate 3 therefore still fails. No reviewed contemporaneous decision document assigns a dollar cost to delaying STS-51-L, a saving from launching on January 28, a quantified schedule value, or an adopted cost/schedule rationale for the reversal. The 1985 CBO shuttle-pricing report is program-wide pricing analysis, not a January 1986 decision record. [4] General schedule pressure, later critique, and post-accident redesign choices cannot be transferred into the missing decision-specific field. Because all five gates are conjunctive, that failure is dispositive.

## Corrections required before publication

1. **Correct the hearing claim.** The evidence record says the post-accident Commission hearing gave no particular dollar amount for redesign. That is false. A witness said the issue had not been “scoped moneywise” but used “maybe $5, $10 million” for extra testing and improvement as an illustrative estimate. This remains insufficient for Gate 3: it was post-accident testimony, hypothetical/unscoped, concerned testing and improvement rather than the January 27 reversal, and was not adopted by the decision-maker. [6]

2. **Do not call the 53 °F control a proven but-for prevention.** Preserve the gate-4 pass as an authoritative link to risk and the January 28 approval counterfactual. Replace any language that says the rejected no-go *caused* the loss, or that “a strong chain” proves the no-go would have avoided all later loss, with this narrower statement.

3. **Narrow the GAO label.** GAO reports that 25 of its counted changes were initiated after 51-L and totalled approximately $1 billion. It explicitly excludes the SRM-joint settlement because it was not a formal change order. Thus “GAO’s counted post-51-L hardware changes” is supported; “formal post-51-L flight-hardware changes” overstates what its reported $1 billion necessarily represents. [7]

4. **State the CBO rounding conflict.** CBO’s summary calls full operational cost $84 million per flight, while its Table 7 reports the NASA-base average operational cost as $85 million, both in 1982 dollars. The record may retain **$84 million** only as the summary figure, with this internal one-million-dollar presentation difference disclosed. Neither number may be used as the cost of a January 1986 delay. [4]

5. **Preserve category type and non-additivity.** The $531 million and $2.1 billion are CBO-reported **budget increases relative to a baseline**, not a price for the decision to launch. The arithmetic is $0.531bn + $2.100bn = **$2.631bn**, which rounds to CBO’s stated **$2.6bn**. The GAO approximately-$1bn change category and the reported $409m-at-cost/$10m-foregone-profit settlement must not be added to $2.631bn without a line-item reconciliation. The contemporaneous newspaper reports the latter terms, while GAO confirms exclusion of the settlement from its table but does not supply the settlement amount. [5] [7] [8]

6. **Clarify scope, horizon, and status labels.** The January 27–FY1987 horizon is defensible as a declared **documented fiscal-category horizon**, not as total social cost, total federal cost, or a valuation of the seven deaths. Federal appropriations are a funding channel, not themselves a discrete downstream harm. The record properly leaves deaths, injuries, indirect mission delay, and third-party costs unmonetized. “Observed” should be reserved for what the cited record reports; the decision counterfactual is **derived from an authoritative finding**, and any causal assertion beyond it is **unresolved**.

## Asymptote and ontology challenge

The three work-order examples cannot support the categorical sentence that no terminal physical system exists. They do, however, leave no **empirically certified** terminal instance under the declared whole-life metric.

A deep geological repository must first be named and bounded; “sealed source in a deep geological repository” is not a single observable case. For the whole-life frame, positive crossings remain during construction and operation: the IAEA says safety assessment covers operational and post-closure periods, and the operational period includes emplacement, construction, backfilling, sealing, closure, regulatory inspection, and radiation monitoring. [15] A safety case may model containment or only radiologically insignificant migration; it is not observation of zero crossings. Canada's regulator further identifies excavation damage zones as possible faster water/gas paths and treats gas/radionuclide transport as a modelled safety-case issue. [16] Thus a whole-life repository is nonterminal once those operational crossings are included. A post-closure, named-facility claim remains model-conditional and cannot be relabelled observed-zero.

For Voyager, a positive crossing remains after correcting the time status. NASA presently documents continued Deep Space Network communication, an X-band telemetry downlink, uplink commands, RTG power, and an intentional embodied message on a gold-plated copper record. [12] [14] The whole-life frame is already nonterminal through launch, flybys, transmissions, and current operation. A hypothetical future “post-radio” frame must be labelled hypothetical; it cannot erase earlier positive crossings, and ongoing thermal/radiative claims require their own stated physical-model basis.

For the black-hole candidate, the phrase “black hole interior past the horizon emits Hawking radiation eventually” is not acceptable as an observed fact and confuses the boundary. NASA describes the horizon as a one-way boundary in the classical account, while observed black-hole systems have external accretion, surrounding emission, gravitational effects, and—in some cases—jets. [17] [18] Hawking emission is a semiclassical theoretical prediction/derivation; analogue-laboratory work is not astronomical observation of radiation from a real black hole. [19] [20] A black hole **as an external system** can have observed positive crossings, subject to an explicit boundary that says whether the accretion disk is inside or outside. The isolated interior candidate cannot be empirically certified, and Hawking radiation must remain **theoretical**, not an observed positive witness.

Finally, the observation argument is logically overclaimed in the work order. An observer obtaining empirical evidence does create an information/energy coupling somewhere, but that shows a limitation on **empirical certification of universal zero**, not that an unobserved terminal physical system is impossible. The preregistered metric correctly separates: no detected crossing; finite-horizon bounds for enumerated channels; and zero upper bounds for every possible channel for all time. Only the third meets the literal falsifier, and it normally requires a complete model plus assumptions rather than measurement alone. [21] The defensible conclusion is therefore: **no whole-life terminal instance is certified by these examples; universal nonexistence is not established; and any terminality proof is conditional on a complete ontology, boundary, horizon, and physical model.**
