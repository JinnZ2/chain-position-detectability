# Validation record — Grenfell zinc-to-PE-ACM cost candidate

**Item:** `grenfell-zinc-to-pe-acm-rainscreen`
**Validation verdict:** **NEEDS CORRECTION — retain as PARTIAL; do not select as a qualifying WO-3 cost case.**
**Publication readiness:** **No**, until the horizon, gate-4 label, product-evidence conflict, and citation ledger are corrected.

## Retained finding

The evidence supports a narrower, publishable finding: during the Grenfell refurbishment, the specification included Proteus HR zinc honeycomb rainscreen panels and invited aluminium-composite-material (ACM) alternatives; cost pressure and value engineering then led to cassette-fixed Reynobond 55 ACM with an unmodified polyethylene (PE) core. The statutory Inquiry records Rydon’s 20 March 2014 value-engineering figures, including **£293,368** for cassette-fixed ACM, and identifies the use of cassette-form unmodified-PE ACM as **primarily responsible for the rapid spread** of the fire. Those are observed documentary and investigative findings, not a demonstrated counterfactual that the exact zinc option would have prevented the fire or every subsequent loss.[1]

The record is also right not to turn the **£134.803m** Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea (RBKC) 2017–19 revenue-expenditure category, the later RBKC accounts, the civil settlement, and national remediation programmes into one total or into a loss caused solely by the £293,368 decision. The records establish real downstream expenditures and transfers, but not a causal allocation to one material choice.[2] [3]

**Result:** gates 1 and 4 fail under the preregistered strict rule. The correct evidence-status label is **PARTIAL / non-qualifying**, not a fully qualifying causal cost case. This is not a finding that the switch was harmless; it is a rejection of an unproved control-specific counterfactual.

## Decision-critical factual and arithmetic checks

The Inquiry supports the decision chronology used in the record: final NBS specification dated 30 January 2014; Rydon’s 20 March value-engineering document; planning approval notified on 31 July; and formal permission on 25 September 2014.[1] It also supports the stated £250,000 and £300,000 early aluminium-for-zinc estimates, Harley’s £419,627 cassette-ACM and £576,973 riveted-ACM estimates, and Rydon’s £293,368 cassette-ACM and £376,175 riveted-ACM figures. These are different commercial presentations, not components of an aggregate.[1]

The RBKC arithmetic is correct: **£56.503m + £41.208m + £11.091m + £18.408m + £7.593m = £134.803m**, also equal to **£86.342m + £48.461m** for the two financial years. The source reports £69.880m in Section 31 grant funding during this period. Therefore £134.803m is gross revenue spend, not an unfunded Council cost or a material-specific loss.[2]

The 2020/21 accounts figures are correctly transcribed: Grenfell Recovery gross expenditure £13.265m less £2.750m income equals £10.515m net; Grenfell Corporate £9.354m less £7.228m equals £2.126m net. The accounts carry an independent true-and-fair opinion.[3] The BBC figure of about £150m across 900 claims is accurately described as secondary reporting of a settlement hearing and must remain corroborative rather than a primary allocated cost.[4]

The historic NAO amounts—£400m social-sector ACM, £200m private-sector ACM, and the announced £1bn non-ACM fund—are accurate **as historic programme commitments** and remain non-additive national categories.[5] The record must not present them as current programme totals: by 2022 the government described a £5.1bn commitment for unsafe-cladding replacement in higher-risk buildings, and the current official guidance says the Building Safety Fund closed to new applications on 1 September 2025 as the Cladding Safety Scheme became the single 11m+ scheme.[6] [7]

## Product evidence and source-version conflict

The record correctly rejects reliance on the cited 2019 Proteus overview as a 2014 certificate for the Grenfell configuration. That overview says the system achieved A2-s1,d0 with the **majority** of facing materials, which is conditional language and is neither an identified 2014 Grenfell product certificate nor a project-specific test.[8]

A material source conflict must now be disclosed. A contemporaneous September 2014 Proteus HR brochure describes the standard system as achieving **Class 0** under BS 476 Parts 6 and 7, while describing an A2 system as **non-standard** and separately available.[9] The later 2019 overview instead describes A2-s1,d0 with a majority of facings.[8] These may reflect a product revision, different test/classification routes, or configuration differences; the available sources do not resolve which. Neither document ties the quoted cost baseline to an exact tested zinc configuration. The conflict reinforces, rather than cures, failure of gate 1.

## Boundary, horizon, and crossing classification

The declared procurement/accounting boundary is usable only if presented as a **stipulated accounting boundary**, not as a physical enclosure. The observed RBKC expenditure is a positive lower-bound witness of a human/institutional burden outside that procurement comparison for the stated finite frame; that suffices to classify the **whole refurbishment system**, not the isolated material substitution, as nonterminal within that frame. It does not establish a monetary crossing rate, a cost allocation, or a complete crossing vector.

The sentence claiming positive witnesses in “several channel families” overreaches its citations. Retain the observed institutional-cost witness—e.g., `L_human/institutional > 0` in the stated currency category—and either cite and quantify each additional matter, information/control, and dependency/exposure witness or label those channels **unresolved**. The nonterminal classification does not require them once one positive supported witness is retained.

The evidence table includes a civil settlement reported in **2023**, but `H` says observation ends with the 2021 accounts and the 2020 NAO report. Correct this by either removing the settlement row from the declared frame or extending `H` explicitly through the 2023 hearing/reporting date. “Whole-life risk boundary” must be renamed **whole-life category map, finite observed horizon**; it is not an observed whole-life terminality assessment. The current government response is dated 26 February 2025 and updated 2 June 2025, whereas the Inquiry’s final Phase 2 report was published 4 September 2024; describe each by its own date and function rather than treating either as a timeless source.[10] [11]

No asymptote is assessed in this cost record, so an “at least one positive crossing after correction” test for an asymptote is **not applicable**. For this case, the independently supported positive institutional-cost witness remains after correction. There is likewise no theoretical physical-model claim to retain. A manufacturer test/certification, if the exact configuration were identified, would be empirical certification under its stated conditions; it would still not prove the model-conditional, but-for claim that the exact control would have prevented all harm, nor prove zero crossings.

## Required edits before use

Change the gate-4 table label from “Met, with causal-scope limit” to **“Partially evidenced; not met as a control-specific gate”**. Add the September 2014 versus September 2019 Proteus source conflict. Align the settlement row and the stated horizon. Recast multi-channel crossing assertions as one observed institutional witness plus unresolved channels unless independently sourced. Delete unused reference [8] in the candidate record’s current reference ledger (its BBC cladding story is not cited in the body), or cite it only for a non-decision-critical contextual claim. Retain the explicit non-addition and no-but-for-all-costs limitations.
