# WO-3 parent source checks

**Checked:** 19 September 2026

These notes preserve direct parent-level checks of decision-critical sources before final synthesis.

## Forsmark excavation-volume conflict

Two current SKB pages disagree on the planned excavated-rock volume. The main project page, last reviewed 17 June 2025, says construction began in January 2025 and states **about 2.7 million m³** over several decades. SKB International’s facility page, last reviewed 25 March 2026, states **about 2.3 million m³**. Both agree on roughly 500 m depth, approximately 12,000 tonnes of spent fuel, 6,000 canisters, and more than 60 km of tunnels. The final study should report the excavation figure as a source conflict or range rather than silently choosing one value.[1] [2]

## Voyager power source

NASA/JPL states that each Voyager carries three radioisotope thermoelectric generators. They convert heat from plutonium-238 decay into electricity. This independently confirms the inventory and continuing decay-heat mechanism, but not the workflow’s derived 2035 heat magnitude, which depends on the separate NASA technical reference and a hypothetical cessation date.[3]

## GW150914

The LIGO Open Science Center records detection at Hanford and Livingston, a false-alarm rate below one per 203,000 years, significance of 5.1 sigma, and radiated energy of 3.0 ± 0.5 solar masses at a 90% credible interval. It reports component masses of 36.2 and 29.1 solar masses, final mass 62.3 solar masses, and final spin 0.68. This supports the nonzero outgoing gravitational-wave witness while preserving its model/posterior status.[4]

## Grenfell value engineering

The Grenfell Tower Inquiry records January 2013 proposals to substitute aluminium panels for zinc for potential savings of £250,000 and £300,000. It states that Studio E viewed changing zinc to a cheaper material as an obvious value-engineering option under pressure from the Tenant Management Organisation. It also records a final NBS specification for Proteus HR zinc honeycomb panels with ACM alternatives, Harley figures of £419,627 for cassette-fixed ACM and £576,973 for riveted ACM, and Rydon’s 20 March 2014 figure of **£293,368** for cassette-fixed ACM. These passages verify the cost-led substitution record. The exact fire classification of the costed Proteus HR zinc baseline remains a separate evidentiary question.[5]
